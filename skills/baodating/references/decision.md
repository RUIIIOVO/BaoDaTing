# Clarification and decision guide

Read this when a vague request needs a choice or when ranking candidates.

## Ask the question that changes the decision

Start with actual low-cost exploration, then build a short menu from observed solution forms. Give 2–4 meaningful directions, their outcomes and tradeoffs, with sourced examples where found. Include “not sure / help me choose / other”; do not force categories to be mutually exclusive.

The most useful next question changes feasibility, candidate scope, or a major cost. Ask one per round. Broad direction often comes first, followed by a mandatory boundary and then actual price tiers, but skip any step the request already answers. Internal subject/function/price fields must not become a user form.

A clarification is unnecessary when its possible answers would leave the recommendation unchanged. A decisive missing fact should remain pending; independent research can continue while awaiting it. If the user asks the agent to choose, use known constraints and explicitly stated assumptions rather than repeating a questionnaire.

## Domain adaptations

These are patterns, not prewritten menus or verified search results. Use actual findings to supply the options.

| Domain | Useful first direction | Detail to verify before a final decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software, plugin, skill, open source | Desired workflow or actual solution form | Compatibility, deployment, license, maintenance, required services and billing |
| Shopping | Use conditions the user can recognize | Fit, exact variant, seller/service, trial/returns, comparable total delivered price |
| Travel | Desired experience and feasible transport burden | Origin, exact dates, people, opening/booking state, realistic transit and refund terms |
| Food | Meal occasion, area, dietary or access needs | Location, opening hours, menu/package conditions, recent reports and reservation needs |
| Research | Field overview, method comparison, reproducible artifacts, or a concrete research problem | Scope, original methods, evidence quality, versions, code/data and reproduction constraints |

If a necessary origin, date or compatibility condition is unknown, ask it when it becomes consequential; do not invent it from a stored personal preference. Do not demand specialist measurements from a shopper when a recognizable use condition or a return trial would be more useful.

## Compare mandatory needs before price

1. Mark each decisive condition as confirmed met, confirmed failed, or unknown, with its evidence. An unknown must-have makes a candidate provisional.
2. Remove confirmed failures from the viable shortlist. When every candidate fails, explain the conflict and explore another form or ask whether a specific constraint can change; do not silently relax a must-have.
3. For feasible candidates, compare valuable outcomes and total costs over the same period. Include necessary subscription/API fees, hosting, setup, learning, maintenance, migration or travel when relevant. Keep uncertain costs explicit.
4. Consider whether one option offers every relevant advantage at no greater cost; preserve genuine differences rather than a contrived alternative.
5. Explain the incremental cost and useful outcome of upgrading. Optional convenience may be worth paying for; a missing mandatory capability cannot be excused by a low price.
6. Give one main recommendation and only useful alternatives. If a decisive unknown remains, recommend the smallest verification step and a conditional decision.

Do not rank by feature count divided by price. Do not apply a fixed “good enough” percentage. Percentages are meaningful only with an explicit need list, justified weighting, and evidence; otherwise use plain descriptions. Scientific evidence is assessed by methods and relevance, not cheapest access.

## Make costs comparable

- **Software:** normalize billing period, seat counts, usage limits, required add-ons and hosting. Free/open-source licensing does not eliminate operation costs. Verify current plan and license instead of inferring them from popularity or repository presence.
- **Goods:** compare the same model/SKU, region, delivery, condition, seller service, warranty and promotion eligibility. Separate unclear transfers or shared access from a verified long-term service.
- **Travel/food:** align dates, people, room/meal/package, transport, taxes, availability and cancellation. A different date or package is not the same quote.
- **Research:** access, model calls, compute and reproduction effort affect feasible study plans. A weaker study does not outrank stronger evidence merely because it is cheaper to obtain.

Treat an unverified bargain as a separate lead with unknown entitlement, duration or service. Do not present it as equivalent to an official offer without checking those conditions.

## Converge and validate economically

Stop when mandatory needs and consequential differences are supported and changing queries no longer changes the shortlist. Do not search every channel to appear thorough. State remaining gaps if access, time or cost prevents adequate verification.

Useful next steps can be a public demo, an authorized trial, checking return terms, verifying a booking condition, or inspecting available reproduction artifacts. Recommend installation, purchase or experiments as next steps when appropriate; only describe them as performed when authorized and actually executed. Continuing with the current solution or postponing a decision is valid.
